chess & pong — direction options
drawn against Amber Arcade, 2026-09-06

Everything below obeys the two rules the hub already settled: chrome against screen — the machine is one amber hue, the board gets whatever colour it needs — and colour by category, so chess borrows jade and pong borrows rose from their hub tiles. Nothing here invents a value; every colour is one already in the palette.

First: the page around the game

Both games currently sit on a bare page — back-link, an h1, a board, a button. The hub got a machine built around it and the game pages did not, which is most of why they read as a different site. The same chrome, one size down, is drawn around every mock below: breadcrumb instead of a back-link, bloomed title, a status strap with the hub's fading cursor, the wash and the scanlines on the chrome only, and the board lifted out from under them. Footer carries the controls hint. That part is shared and is not one of the options.

Chess

The hard question design/TODO.md flags is the two square colours. It gets easier once you notice it is really two questions, and that they are independent — you can take the board from one option and the pieces from another:

the boardthe two armies
Two-tone ramp — two steps of the amber ramp, near-black. Reads as a board, adds no hue.
Tinted two-tone — the same two steps pulled toward jade, so the board says "strategy".
Vector grid — no fills at all; hairlines and a 5% amber wash on the dark squares.		 Solid vs hollow — Unicode already ships both sets (♚ filled, ♔ outline); one army each.
Two hues — amber army, jade army, both solid.
VT323 letters — K Q R B N P upper for white, lower for black.
Stroke SVG — the hub's own line-icon vocabulary, 6 glyphs.

One finding that constrains this: VT323 has no chess glyphs. Its two subsets stop at Latin Extended, so ♔–♟ fall through to whatever the OS serves — which is the soft serif you can see in the current screenshot, and it is a different typeface on every machine. Options A and B accept a second face on the chess page and should name it; options C and D make the problem disappear.

  • White is the solid glyph, black is the outline one — the inversion of the Unicode convention, on purpose: on a dark ground the bright army has to be the filled one or "white" stops reading as white. Same trick DESIGN.md already banked for Minesweeper: when hue runs out, change the mark.

C · Vector Grid

WHITE to move

click a piece, then a squarepawns auto-promote · castling & en passant
  • The board is drawn, not filled — a hairline every square and a 5% amber wash on the dark ones. It is the only option that looks like a vector display rather than a chess set photographed at night.
  • Six stroke pieces on the hub's 48×48 grid, one weight, currentColor — the same vocabulary as the six tile icons, so the site has one drawing hand. No typeface problem, and the pieces stay crisp at any size.
  • Most work of the four: six glyphs to draw and tune. Also the most distinctive, and the pieces are reusable — a captured-piece tray, a promotion picker, the hub tile.
  • Captured pieces sit on the board’s own edges — what Black has taken along the top, what White has taken along the bottom, each on the side of the player who took them. No labels: the pieces are jade or amber, which already says whose they were, and the edge says who took them. They are the same six drawings at 26px, held at 42% of their army’s hue with the glow off, because a taken piece is spent and should not compete with the live board an inch away.
  • One number on the whole board. Only the side that is ahead carries a figure, and it is the difference — +3 here, on White’s edge. Two running totals would be two numbers to subtract before learning the one thing a player reads for, and the losing side showing nothing is itself the fastest way to say who is losing. Pieces are ordered Q R B N P, the way every chess interface orders them.
  • The trays are why this board shows a middlegame while the other three show an opening: an opening has no captures, and an empty tray demonstrates nothing. Values are the standard 9/5/3/3/1.
  • Watch: outlined pieces on an unfilled board is a lot of hairline. If it reads as thin, the fix is a faint fill inside the white army, not a heavier stroke.

  • One typeface on the whole page, which no other option can claim. Uppercase amber is white, lowercase jade is black — the ASCII-board convention, and it is unambiguous at any size.
  • Free to build and impossible to break. It is also the most demanding on the player: a N is not a horse, and someone who plays casually reads shapes faster than letters.
  • Worth showing to a chess player before choosing it. My read: it is the most on-brand and the least playable, which usually means it belongs as an option rather than as the default.

Anime Pong

Pong needs less than chess. The court and the lightning already look like this site; what does not are the rounded pill buttons, the unframed board floating on black, and an ability meter that is three unlabelled blocks in a corner. Three changes, and they stack — you can take all three or any one.

What was chosen

  • Chess: direction C, the vector grid with stroke pieces — settled by Gabriel, along with two of the six drawings. The knight is the angular one, straight lines only, picked over three rounder alternatives. The king is Broad: the domed head, collar band and wide base, picked after two further sets were rejected outright for reading as tombstones and worse. Both rejections were about the silhouette rather than the detail, which is the note to carry into the four pieces nobody has looked at yet.
  • Pong: all three, in that order. 1 is the only one that is a real defect (the pills), 2 settles an open question and deletes a stray colour, 3 is polish. Half of 2 already exists — the player paddle reads the hub’s rose rather than --win, with the affected assertion in tests/pong.test.js rewritten alongside it. What is left of 2 is the opponent’s half.
  • Still open on chess: the rook, bishop, queen and pawn have never been reviewed, and the capture trays are new page furniture with ids of their own.

Open, for whoever builds it

  • Does the game-page chrome become shared CSS? Five other games need the same frame. It probably belongs beside hub.css as something like game.css rather than copied six times — but that is a decision about the system, not about chess.
  • If chess ships a second typeface for the glyphs, which one, and is it self-hosted? The font rule in DESIGN.md is emphatic about not using the CDN; a chess-glyph face is 20–40K.
  • Board size on a phone. Eight squares of 54px plus gutters does not fit 375px; the coordinates are the first thing to drop.
  • Does the move log exist at all? It is drawn in B because it is the thing this typeface is best at, but it is new game scope, and no other game here has a side panel.
